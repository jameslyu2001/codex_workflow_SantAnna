# Academic writing style

- Lead with the claim, then supply evidence. Remove throat-clearing and generic importance claims.
- Prefer active voice and concrete subjects. Use short sentences for difficult ideas.
- Match causal language to the identification argument; describe correlations as associations.
- Define notation at first use and keep it consistent across text, equations, tables, and figures.
- Give every number units, uncertainty where relevant, and a traceable source such as a table, equation, script, or cited paper.
- Keep one main point per paragraph. Attribute prior results to specific sources rather than writing “the literature shows.”
- Avoid stacked transitions, decorative tricolons, em-dash overuse, symmetric boilerplate, inflated adjectives, and sycophantic framing.
- Never invent citations, magnitudes, results, or institutional facts. Use `[CITE]`, `[NUM]`, or an explicit unresolved note when evidence is absent.

Before finalizing, distinguish verified claims, working assumptions, author judgment, and unresolved questions. Preserve the author's substantive voice rather than homogenizing it.
